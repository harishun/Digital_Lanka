Real-World Issues to Address


Connectivity and Infrastructure

Network Dead Zones
• Traffic stops often occur in areas with poor cellular coverage, preventing the app from verifying data or refreshing expired access.
• An offline-first architecture or encrypted QR code fallback is required.

Device Failure
• If a user’s phone battery dies, they cannot present their digital documents.
• Officers need a secondary lookup method (e.g., searching by license plate or NIC) that does not rely on the user's device.


Vehicle using privileges Edge Cases

Casual Delegation
• Forcing users to manually grant access for valets, mechanics, or a neighbor moving the car creates high user friction.
Emergency Situations
• If a medical emergency occurs and an unauthorized person drives the vehicle, the system might automatically flag them, leading to unwarranted police interception.


Security and Abuse of Power

Administrative Oversight
• The "Override any activity" privilege for Administrators is a severe security risk.
• The system must enforce immutable audit logs so that every admin action is permanently recorded and traceable.
Officer Authentication
• Allowing officers to self-register with public batch numbers invites impersonation.
• Officer accounts must be centrally provisioned by the police department's IT directory.

Data Privacy
• Officers having access to citizens' home addresses and travel history requires strict monitoring to prevent profiling or stalking.

Operational Bottlenecks & Detainment

Document Fraud
• Relying on users to upload photos of documents requires manual human verification to catch manipulated images.

False Detainment
• If a user recovers their own stolen vehicle but forgets to update the app, they risk being held at gunpoint by police relying on outdated system data.
